Types of Amazon Ads and How They Actually Work

When you first log into Amazon Ads and see the campaign creation screen, it looks simple enough. Three options. How complicated can it be?

Pretty complicated, actually. Running the wrong ad type for what you're trying to do costs money and burns time before you figure out which one to use and when. Here's a breakdown that cuts through the noise.

In this article

  • 2. Sponsored Brands — Once You Have Something to Show

Ad Type 01

Sponsored Products

✦ Best for Beginners · Highest ROI · Start Here

This is the one Amazon highlights first on their own campaign selection screen — and there's a good reason for that. Sponsored Products are the ads that show up directly inside search results. They look almost exactly like organic listings — same image, same title, same price, same star rating — with only a tiny "Sponsored" label.

Buyers don't really notice that label. They just see a relevant product and click.

⚡ How it works
→
You pick a product and choose keywords — or let Amazon pick them automatically via Auto campaigns
→
You set a bid. When someone searches, your product competes in a real-time auction for a top spot
→
You only pay when someone clicks — cost-per-click model
→
You can see exactly which search terms triggered your ad, which converted, and which wasted money
💡
The feedback loop with Sponsored Products is tight and clean. It's one product at a time, so the data is reliable. Run auto campaigns first to discover keywords, then switch to manual campaigns once you know what converts. Don't touch Sponsored Brands or Display until you've done at least 3–6 months of Sponsored Products first.
Ad Type 02

Sponsored Brands

✦ For Brand Discovery · Multiple Products · Established Sellers

Sponsored Brands are the banner-style ads at the very top of Amazon search results — the large ones with your logo, a custom headline, and two or three products displayed side by side. More visible, more brand-forward, and more expensive.

Amazon describes them as "driving brand discovery," which is accurate. These ads are less about pushing a single product and more about putting your brand name in front of people who haven't heard of you yet.

🔷 When Sponsored Brands actually work
→
You have multiple solid products with strong review counts
→
You have a recognisable logo and a real brand story to tell
→
You want to own the top of search for your product category
→
You've already validated conversions through Sponsored Products first
⚠️
The most common mistake: sellers set up Sponsored Brands before they're ready. Running Sponsored Brands with two products, a generic logo, and no real brand story means you'll get clicks but very few conversions. Build your catalog and reviews first. Then come back to this.
Ad Type 03

Display, Video & Audio

✦ Advanced · High Budget · Awareness & Retargeting

This one is fundamentally different from the other two. Sponsored Products and Sponsored Brands show your ads to people who are actively searching on Amazon. They already have intent.

Display ads reach people everywhere else — other websites, apps, streaming services, and back on Amazon as they browse. You're targeting based on past behaviour and interests, not what someone is actively searching for right now.

🎬 What's inside this category
→
Display ads — Follow users across the internet based on their Amazon browsing behaviour
→
Video ads — Play before or during Amazon streaming content (Prime Video, Twitch)
→
Audio ads — Play on Alexa devices and Amazon Music — pure awareness tools
→
Retargeting — Show ads specifically to people who viewed your product listing but didn't buy
💡
ROI here is harder to measure — you're planting a seed, not catching someone mid-search. The sellers who use Display well are either large brands or sellers with very specific retargeting goals. For most sellers starting out, this can wait. The cost is highest and the results are the least direct.
Decision Guide

So Which One Should You Use?

Here's how to think about it based on where your business actually is right now — not where you want to be.

Your Stage
What to Run
Just launched
Under 50 reviews
Sponsored Products only. Auto campaign first, then manual once you have keyword data. Keep it simple — don't add complexity before you understand your conversion rate.
Sponsored Products
Established
Growing catalog, 50–200+ reviews
Add Sponsored Brands to start building brand recognition. Keep your Sponsored Products campaigns running alongside — don't replace one with the other.
Sponsored Products Sponsored Brands
Strong brand
Multiple products, larger budget
Now you can experiment with Display — especially retargeting people who visited your listings but didn't convert. This is where the advanced stuff starts to pay off.
Sponsored Products Sponsored Brands Display
🎯
The mistake most sellers make is trying to run everything at once before they have the budget or the data to do it right. Amazon's ad platform rewards patience and optimisation — not spray-and-pray.
The Bottom Line

One Last Thing

The campaign type is just the starting point. What actually determines whether your ads make money is what happens after you launch — how you read the data, which keywords you cut, how you adjust bids, and whether your listing converts the traffic you're buying.

The real sequence that works 🎯
1
Get your listing right first — strong title, bullets, images, and A+ content before spending a rupee on ads
2
Then run the ads — start with Sponsored Products auto campaigns to collect keyword data
3
Then learn from the numbers — cut wasted spend, scale what converts, and only add complexity when you've earned it

A bad listing with a great campaign type still loses money. A great listing with even a basic Sponsored Products campaign can do very well. That's really the whole game.
