The Starting Point

My first paid campaign — and it wasn't working

This was my first-ever sales campaign on Meta Ads. I was running a top-of-funnel campaign for an e-commerce client — and it looked fine on the surface. Ads were running. Money was going out. But the numbers told a different story.

With around ₹8,000 spent, the campaign had generated only about ₹3,000 in return. That's a ROAS of roughly 0.37x — spending more than ₹2 for every ₹1 made. For any business, that's not a campaign. That's a leak.

Instead of shutting it down or throwing more budget at it, I decided to do what should always come first: understand why it wasn't working.

Step 01

Diagnosing the Campaign — Finding the real problem

I went through the campaign with a structured audit mindset — examining every layer: creative, audience, placement, bid strategy, landing page alignment, and funnel fit. Not just looking at spend and results, but asking why each metric looked the way it did.

⚠ Issues Identified

  • Creative was misaligned to TOF intent — too much product push, too little awareness hook
  • Audience targeting was broad without layered signals, reaching people unlikely to convert
  • Ad sets were competing against each other, cannibalising the budget internally
  • Landing page had friction — slow load speed and messaging mismatch with the ad
  • Bid strategy and optimisation event were mismatched for a cold audience
  • No structured testing — multiple creatives running simultaneously with no controlled comparison

Each issue on its own is manageable. Combined, they were quietly draining the budget with no clear purchase path for the user — multiple small inefficiencies compounding into one large problem.

Step 02

The Optimisation Plan — Fixing each layer systematically

Once the issues were mapped, I built a clear plan before touching anything. Changing multiple variables at once without a plan is how campaigns get worse, not better. I worked through each problem area in order of impact.

Creative overhaul: Rewrote ad copy and restructured creatives to lead with a hook relevant to a cold audience — problem-aware, not product-first. Stop the scroll first, then earn the click.

Audience restructuring: Removed overlapping audiences and rebuilt ad sets with clearly defined segments. Each ad set had a specific audience hypothesis and enough budget to gather meaningful data before making decisions.

Bid and event alignment: Shifted the optimisation event to better match where cold audiences actually are in the funnel — so Meta's algorithm optimised for achievable actions, not aspirational ones.

Structured creative testing: Set up a controlled testing framework where one variable changed at a time — making it possible to know exactly what was working.

Landing page coordination: Flagged load time and messaging issues to the client team. When a user clicks an ad and lands on a mismatched page, the journey breaks — regardless of how good the ad is.

Step 03

Execution — Running the revised campaigns

With the plan in place, I executed changes across the full campaign structure. New ad sets launched with clean audiences. Revised creatives went live. Budget was redistributed toward ad sets showing early signals of efficiency.

Results — April 2026

From 0.37x ROAS to 5x ROAS

After diagnosis, restructuring, and consistent execution, the numbers shifted completely. With ₹28,000 in total ad spend, the campaigns generated ₹80,000+ in sales — a return on ad spend of 5x.

This wasn't a lucky spike. It was the result of fixing the right things in the right order — and giving the revised campaigns enough room to optimise.

Key Takeaways

What this project taught me

A bad ROAS is rarely a single problem. This campaign had multiple compounding inefficiencies — and fixing them required diagnosis first, action second. Every decision was traceable to a specific finding. Nothing was guesswork.

01

Always audit before optimising

Changing things without knowing why they're failing is a gamble. The audit made every subsequent action purposeful.

02

ROAS is a symptom, not the disease

Low ROAS pointed to deeper issues — creative mismatch, audience overlap, funnel friction. Treating the symptom alone wouldn't have worked.

03

Creatives must match the audience's mindset

Cold traffic doesn't respond to the same messaging as warm audiences. TOF creative needs to earn attention, not assume it.

04

Structure prevents budget waste

Overlapping ad sets compete against each other in the auction. Clean structure directly affects efficiency — it's not optional.

05

Test one variable at a time

Without a controlled testing framework, you can't know what's working. Structured tests turn campaign data into actionable insight.

06

Scale only after efficiency is proven

Increasing budget on a broken campaign accelerates the loss. Scaling the restructured campaign is what delivered ₹80K+ in return.
